## As-Built

### Current Production Economy

All facts below are measured from `src/` at `0863cbc`; the executable evidence
is `tests/productionEconomyMeasurement.test.ts`.

**Entities and rates**

| Building | Workforce | Input | Output | Rate | Recurring cost | Cap | Access |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Residence | 0 | none | housing capacity | 1 | none | n/a | none |
| Farm | 1 | none | Food | 2/tick | none | none (uncapped) | road |
| Well | 1 | none | Water | 2/tick | none | none (uncapped) | road |
| Workshop | 1 | none | Material | 2/tick | 1 Material/tick when staffed | 25 production inflow per operational Workshop | road |

Step 10CQ **income** (not production) is a second Material inflow: Farm worker
+1, Well worker +1, Workshop worker +2 Material/tick, credited before commands
and bypassing the production cap. Storage hub capacities: Food 50, Water 30,
Material 40, of which 15 Material is the protected floor (Step 10BJ); only
Material overflow ever enters the hub.

**Production flow (existing)**

```text
Workforce (job assignment, mobility-gated)
   -> Farm / Well / Workshop output, road-gated, staffing-gated
   -> Food / Water / Material stocks
   -> Food & Water consumed by colonists (needs)
   -> Material spent by construction (placeBuilding / placeRoads)
   -> population / settlement progression
```

**Phase 7 flow (frozen)**

```text
employment -> Material income -> Material stock
           -> construction / roads / protected-reserve affordability
```

Separated concepts (not interchangeable today):

- **production** — building output (`getMaterialProductionPerTick`, gross);
- **income** — per-employed-colonist Material (`getWorkforceIncome`), not production;
- **stock** — canonical `resources.food/water/construction`;
- **expenditure** — `placeBuilding` (25, +1 Water for a Workshop), `placeRoads` (5/cell);
- **consumption** — Food 1/colonist/tick, Water 1/served-colonist/tick;
- **capacity** — housing capacity, job capacity, Material storage (25/Workshop), hub caps;
- **affordability** — `getPlacementAffordability` / `getRoadsPlacementAffordability`.

**Measured base facts**

- No building definition carries an input/recipe/efficiency field; keys are
  exactly `constructionTicks, housingCapacity, constructionCost, constructionWaterCost`.
- Road access gates every producer (0 output without a road, nominal with).
- Staffing gates production and upkeep together (vacant Workshop: 0 / 0).
- Balanced colony (Farm + Well, 2 colonists): Food and Water are at exact
  break-even (2 produced = 2 consumed / served); Material still accrues **+2/tick**
  purely from income — no Workshop exists in that colony.
- Food and Water are uncapped in the simulation; a Farm colony reaches Food 60
  and a Well colony Water 60 by tick 60 with the hub untouched (`storageMaterial 0`).
- Material production is capped by `25 x operational Workshops`; income bypasses
  the cap, and overflow above the cap is retained by the hub (Material only).
- A Workshop below cap adds `stored 2 + income 2 - upkeep 1 = +3` Material/tick;
  at the cap `stored 0`, so the stock still grows from income while the hub
  captures the overflow.
- Material production consumes no resource input; the single recurring cost in
  the whole economy is the staffed-Workshop upkeep of 1 Material/tick.
- A workforce-constrained colony (3 workplaces, 1 colonist) employs exactly 1
  colonist; the other two workplaces stay vacant and produce nothing. Job income
  differs by job: Farm 1, Well 1, Workshop 2.

### Measurements

The six representative settlements traced at horizons 1/4/12/24/60
(`low-population farm`, `balanced farm+well`, `production-heavy`,
`workforce-constrained`, `stock-rich`, `stock-poor`) produce these headline
facts:

| Settlement | Population | Food/tick | Water/tick | Material/tick | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| low-population farm | 1 | +1 net (2 - 1) | 0 | +1 income | Food uncapped |
| balanced farm+well | 2 | 0 (break-even) | 0 (break-even) | +2 income | no Workshop |
| production-heavy | 2 | +2 net (4 - 2) | 0 | +2 income | 4 workplaces, 2 vacant |
| workforce-constrained | 1 | +1 net | 0 | +1 income | 2 vacant workplaces |
| stock-rich | 1 | +1 net | 0 | +1 income | hub fixed at 40 |
| stock-poor | 1 | +1 net | 0 | +1 income | generic constraints only |

`production-heavy` demonstrates the binding constraint directly: 4 workplaces
and 2 colonists means half the production capacity is idle, regardless of
stocks. `stock-rich` shows that extra Material neither accelerates nor changes
any decision — the hub is already full and the stock simply grows.

### Candidate Directions

No direction is ranked. Each is measured against the existing system.

**A — Production inputs.** No input exists. Every producer turns labour into
output with zero resource consumption; the only recurring cost is the Workshop
upkeep (a Material deduction, not a production input). A real input economy
would require either a new resource or a new sink before it could exist.
Would it create a new decision? Only if the input competes with something else
the player already does; today there is nothing to compete with, so it would be
speculative complexity.

**B — Production chains.** There are exactly three resources and three
producers, and no building consumes another building's output. Colonists are
the only consumers of Food/Water; construction is the only consumer of
Material. Introducing intermediate goods would add resources and buildings
before any measured bottleneck demands one.

**C — Explicit consumption.** Already exists: Food 1/colonist/tick
(all-or-nothing feeding, starvation removes colonists) and Water
1/served-colonist/tick (coverage/admission gate). Both already have distinct
consequences, so "add consumption" would duplicate an existing flow unless it
introduces a new good.

**D — Industrial specialization.** Already exists as the three-way workforce
allocation: one colonist holds one job, and the job determines the output
(Food/Water/Material) and the income (1/1/2). A new specialization layer would
repeat the existing allocation trade-off rather than create a new one.

**E — Efficiency / upgrades.** No efficiency, multiplier or upgrade state
exists anywhere (not on `SimulationState`, not on definitions). Upgrade
systems are the roadmap's Phase 12 (Technology / specialization), not Phase 8.

### Decision Analysis

Using the established decision-quality standard (`docs/roadmap/Step10CA.md`,
`Step10CL.md`, `Step10CU.md`): a Phase 8 mechanic is justified only if it
creates a player decision that does not exist today.

- Today the player decides: which job each colonist takes (Food vs Water vs
  Material, with different income), what to build with Material, whether to
  spend the protected reserve, and when to run construction crews. Those are
  live decisions backed by measured constraints.
- Directions A and B would add inputs/chains that no measured bottleneck
  demands. Direction C duplicates existing colonist consumption. Direction D
  duplicates the existing three-way allocation. Direction E belongs to
  Phase 12.
- The recurring anti-feature pattern ("more resource counters, passive
  multipliers, arbitrary upkeep, linear scaling, fake scarcity, timers,
  maintenance meters, UI accounting without a decision") is not avoided by any
  candidate on current evidence.

### Decision Gate

**Outcome B — DEFER.**

No production-economy mechanic currently passes the new-decision threshold. The
current model already has: three distinct produced goods with real consumers;
two independent Material inflows (production and income); a bounded production
stock with a retention hub; a protected reserve; and a workforce-allocation
trade-off. Nothing measured shows a decision the player cannot already make.

Reopening evidence required before Phase 8 implementation is reconsidered
(carried forward from `Step10CL.md` and extended by this measurement):

1. a **measured, player-visible shortage or surplus** that the existing
   three-way reallocation repertoire provably cannot restore; or
2. a demonstrated **intermediate good / input** whose consumption creates a
   decision not expressible through Food/Water/Material allocation and
   construction; or
3. evidence that a producer's output has **no sink** and therefore no decision
   beyond stock accumulation (today Food/Water/Material all have sinks:
   colonists and construction).

Until then, do not invent a Phase 8 mechanic.
